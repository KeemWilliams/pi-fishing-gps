Raspberry Pi fishing GPS: an offline DIY chartplotter project

I am building a fishing GPS for my own use around a Raspberry Pi 5. It keeps working with no signal, shows depth and tidal current on a map, and tells me what it knows and what it is only guessing.

Last changed 2026-10-28

What this is

The Pi fishing GPS is a handheld, offline-first fishing computer. A Raspberry Pi 5 runs the services and serves a touch interface to its own 7 inch display. A phone can join the Pi's Wi-Fi and open the same interface.

The software side is well along on a laptop. The hardware side has not started: I have not bought the Pi, and nothing has been tried on the water. The roadmap page separates what is built from what is planned, and each page carries a label for its own part.

What it does so far

  • Shows an offline map with depth bands, contour lines and depth numbers, built with MapLibre. See map and depth charts.
  • Draws NOAA predicted tidal currents as one arrow per station, with a speed and a flood or ebb marker. See tidal currents.
  • Shows wind as a small compass on the map and as a dial on the Today screen. See wind.
  • Rates the bite from water temperature, boat wind and a few other inputs, using weights I re-fitted against 58,254 coastal Virginia fishing trips. See bite score.
  • Ranks species candidates from a photo with a model that runs on the Pi. See fish ID.
  • Answers typed questions from its own data, and says where each answer came from. See voice assistant.

What it does not do

It does not navigate, it does not replace a marine radio or a weather forecast from the National Weather Service, and it has not been tested on a real boat. The depth data in every screenshot here is invented for the demo. The safety and limits page lists the rest.

How it is built

The Pi side is Python: a handful of small services that talk over a local message broker and store data in SQLite. The interface is Svelte 5 with MapLibre, served from the Pi. Maps and data packs are built on a laptop, signed, and copied over, so the Pi never needs to download map data in the field. Screenshots on this site are captured automatically from the interface running on demo data. The text is written and edited by hand.

Every number from outside sources is credited on the data sources and credits page.

Explore the project

  • Hardware Planned

    The software runs on a laptop with fake sensors. This page describes the hardware it is written for and what I still have to buy and prove.

  • Map and depth Built

    The map is a MapLibre vector map that runs from files on the Pi. It shows depth as colour bands, contour lines and numbers, and it carries a Not for navigation notice at the bottom of the screen.

  • Tidal currents Built

    The map draws NOAA's predicted tidal current as an arrow at each current station, with a speed and a flood or ebb marker. Every one of them says Predicted, because none of it is measured at the boat.

  • Wind Built

    Wind shows up in two places: one compass on the map, and a dial on the Today screen. Both say whether the number is a forecast or a measurement, and neither pretends the wind was measured at the boat.

  • Bite score Built

    The bite score started as the common recipe of solunar windows, moon phase and barometer trend. I tested those inputs against 58,254 recorded fishing trips from coastal Virginia and kept only what the data supported.

  • Fish ID Partly built

    A small image model ranks candidate species from a fish photo, entirely on the device. It can also say it is not sure. It never decides: you pick the species.

  • Voice assistant Partly built

    You can ask the unit questions such as what the water temperature is or what is biting. The answer is built from the device's own data, checked against it before it is shown, and listed with its sources.

  • Data and credits

    Almost everything this project knows comes from public data and open-source software that other people built. This page says what is used where, and under what terms I could find.

  • Safety and limits

    This is a hobby project. It is not for navigation, it has not been tested on the water, and some of what it shows is a prediction or a guess. This page lists the limits in plain terms.

  • Roadmap

    Three labels are used here and nowhere else on the site. Built means it works on a laptop with demo data. Partly built means some pieces exist. Planned means it is not started or it needs hardware I do not have.

Frequently asked questions

Can I download or buy the Pi fishing GPS?

No. The application code is private and the project is built for my own use, so there is nothing to install or order. This site documents what I have built, what the data showed and what is still planned.

Does it work without a phone signal?

That is the design. The map, depth data, tide and current predictions and the species information are stored on the Pi. Forecasts and weather alerts need a connection, so the app shows how old they are and says when a forecast may be wrong.

Can I navigate a boat with it?

No. It is not a chart plotter in the safety sense, it is not for navigation, and the maps on this site are a made-up demo river. Depth figures are labelled with an unknown datum. Use proper charts and a marine radio.

What hardware does a DIY Raspberry Pi fishing GPS need?

The plan is a Raspberry Pi 5, a 7 inch touch display, a GPS receiver, a power bank, a buzzer for alerts and a dry bag for the first trips. Water temperature, pressure, an LTE modem and a Ping2 sonar are optional additions. I have not bought the Pi yet, so everything so far runs on a laptop with demo data. The hardware page has the details.

Does the moon or solunar table predict the bite?

In the data I tested, no. I checked 58,254 recreational fishing trips from coastal Virginia. Moon phase and solunar windows showed no detectable effect on whether a trip caught a fish, and any effect larger than about 2 points is ruled out. Water temperature was the clearest signal. The bite score page has the chart.

Can the fish identifier name what I caught?

It ranks candidates from 43 species and can say it is not sure. On photos it had not seen, its top guess was right 82.1% of the time. It never saves a species by itself: you confirm the species, and you still check the size and creel limits.

Can I use the fish identification model commercially?

No. The model was trained partly on photos licensed CC BY-NC, so it is for non-commercial use only. A commercial version would need retraining without those photos.

Why Virginia and the Chesapeake Bay?

That is where I fish, and it is where the public data is dense: tide and current stations, buoys, a long-running recreational fishing survey and state records. The software is not tied to the region, but the data I have checked is.

Not for navigation. This is a personal hobby project. Every map and number on this site is demo data or a prediction. Do not use it to steer a boat, and carry proper charts and a marine radio.
