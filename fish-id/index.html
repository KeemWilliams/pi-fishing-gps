Fish identification on a Raspberry Pi

A small image model ranks candidate species from a fish photo, entirely on the device. It can also say it is not sure. It never decides: you pick the species.

Partly built The model is trained and tested on a laptop. It is not installed on a Pi, and the demo interface has no photo screen yet. · Last changed 2026-10-28

What it does

You give it a photo of a fish. It returns up to three candidate species with a confidence band, or "not sure" when its top answer is the catch-all Other class or the confidence is low. The app shows the candidates as a list and nothing is written to the catch log until you tap one. If the model is not installed, the app falls back to a manual species picker.

The model recognises 43 species that anglers meet in Virginia fresh and salt water, plus an Other class that works as a gate for fish and objects outside that list. It runs on the Pi's CPU through ONNX Runtime and needs no network. No photo leaves the device.

Look-alikes

Some pairs are easy to confuse and carry different rules: striped bass and white perch, blue and channel catfish, red and black drum, the shads and river herrings, and the two sunfish groups. When two members of one of these groups are close, the app caps its confidence at low and says which pair is in doubt, because a wrong high-confidence answer could send you to the wrong size limit. The card always carries a reminder: "Verify before keeping a fish. Size and creel limits depend on the species."

How good is it

These numbers are from the laptop evaluation of the current model, called v2. The test photos were never used in training, and the split was made by observation so that photos from one sighting never land on both sides.

MeasureResult
Top-1 on 5,850 known test photos82.1%
Average over species (macro top-1)80.8%
Correct and not rejected, at the shipped gate80.5%
Unseen-species fish sent to "Other" (2,375 photos)39.2%
Non-fish photos sent to "Other" (236 photos)72.5%
Real target fish wrongly sent to "Other"5.0%

The weak spot is fish that are not in the 43 but look like one that is. At the shipped setting, 39% of those come back as Other and the rest get a guess, which is why every result is a candidate list that you confirm. The gate could be set to reject 73.5% of unseen fish, but it would also throw out 19.4% of the fish I do want it to recognise, so I left it where it is.

Some species are weaker than the average: American shad (55.6% in testing), blueback herring (15.0%, from only 20 test photos), bowfin (60.5%), and rainbow trout (68.2%). These are the species I would double check by eye.

How it was trained

The base is EfficientNet-B0, pretrained on ImageNet, taken from the timm library and fine-tuned at 256 by 256 pixels for 18 epochs on a laptop with an RTX 4070 GPU. Training took about 59 minutes. The exported model is a 16.3 MB ONNX file. On the laptop it runs one image in about 21 ms on one thread. I have not timed it on a Pi 5.

The training photos come from iNaturalist: research-grade, non-captive observations, 60,885 photos in all. I kept only photos under the licences CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC and CC BY-NC-SA, and dropped any with a no-derivatives licence. At most 10% of a species' photos come from one observer and at most three from one observation. Downloaded photos are decoded and re-encoded fresh, which also strips their metadata.

Non-commercial only

55,103 of those 60,885 photos (91%) carry a non-commercial licence. So the model is for personal, non-commercial use, and a commercial product would need a retrained model without those photos. Each photo's author and licence are recorded in a dataset manifest that I keep with the model files. It is 23 MB and not published on this site.

What is not done

  • The model is not installed on a Pi, because I do not have one.
  • The demo interface has no photo identification screen. The species cards in the screenshot below are the catalogue the classifier maps its answers onto, and the demo catalogue has only six species.
  • The camera program does not exist. Photos would come from an import for now.
  • The fish length from a photo idea, using a printed measuring board, is approved but not started.
