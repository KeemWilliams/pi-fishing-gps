Fishing bite forecast: what 58,254 Virginia trips showed

The bite score started as the common recipe of solunar windows, moon phase and barometer trend. I tested those inputs against 58,254 recorded fishing trips from coastal Virginia and kept only what the data supported.

Built The weights are a judgement based on trip data, not a fitted model, and they are not yet checked against my own catches. · Last changed 2026-10-28

The short answer

I asked one question of each input: does it change the chance that a fishing trip catches anything? The outcome was whether a trip caught at least one fish, kept or released. 58,254 trips from NOAA's Marine Recreational Information Program (MRIP) had a known result, and 72.0% of them caught something. The main model used 58,133 trips on 3,197 dates.

  1. Water temperature was the only robust effect, and most of it is season. Catch chance rose from about 45% at 6 to 10 degrees C to about 80% at 24 degrees and above. Within the same month, a warm day against a cool one added 5.2 points (95% interval +3.4 to +6.6).
  2. Moon phase, solunar windows, air pressure level and the 24 hour pressure change showed no detectable effect. The moon came out at +0.4 points (interval -1.7 to +2.1) and the solunar windows at +0.2 (-1.6 to +1.8). The data rule out a moon or solunar effect larger than about 2 points.
  3. Wind cost a few points on boat trips and nothing on shore and pier trips. Calm was no better than moderate wind.
  4. The sunrise and sunset bump is mostly a quirk of when trips are recorded. It shrinks to roughly 5 points once trip timing is controlled for, with wide uncertainty.
  5. How you fish matters far more than any of these. Charter and head boats caught something on 85.5% of trips, private boats on 70.7%, piers on 62.1% and other shore trips on 48.5%.

Water temperature

The curve rises and does not peak. For flounder, striped bass, croaker, seatrout, red drum, cobia, spot and black sea bass the curves also rise and none peaks, so the old idea of a species "optimum band" got no support. Trips inside the app's old optimum band caught slightly less than trips outside it (odds ratio 0.86, interval 0.76 to 0.98).

Temperature stands in for season here. When I put month and temperature in the model together, held-out accuracy improved a little (log loss 0.4812, against 0.4828 for month alone), so temperature carries information beyond the calendar, though not a lot.

Everything else, side by side

The chart shows the change in catch chance as an input moves from its 10th to its 90th percentile, with other inputs and controls held fixed. The controls were month, year, fishing mode, target species, hours fished, area and county group.

Two details are easy to miss. First, tide range correlates 0.33 with moon strength inside a month, so a tide effect could hide a moon effect or the other way round. The model fits them together, and neither shows anything. Second, I also ran a weaker check on tag, recapture and trophy citation records, which only show catches and never blank days. Moon and tide range were null in every source, which matches the trip data. Wind and pressure looked stronger there, but that source mixes angler effort with fish behaviour, so I do not lean on it.

What the score does now

The score is 50 plus a few effects, kept between 0 and 100. I changed the effects to match the findings above.

Moon, solunar and pressure are still computed and still shown on the screen, labelled "shown for information, not scored". The spoken summary only quotes inputs that moved the score, so the assistant can no longer say the moon helped.

The band cut-offs had to move too. With the new weights the score runs from about 21 to 63, so the old cut-offs of 65 and 80 would never be reached. The new ones are fair at 40, good at 55 and prime at 62, set from the observed catch rate.

Prime is rare on purpose: about 1% of trips. Reaching 62 needs the warmest water plus a sunrise or sunset bonus, or an ocean front, a chlorophyll edge or your own catches.

Where this is weak

  • The weights are judgement informed by the data, not a fitted model. Check them against my own catch log once it holds about 100 trips.
  • MRIP records the end of a trip and a county, not an hour or a spot. The data cannot say anything about the time of a bite, the tide stage within a trip or the three hour pressure trend.
  • There are no January or February Virginia trips in the data and very few in March, so the slow band in winter is a reading of the curve and not a measured catch rate.
  • Clarity, lure and rain were not in the data. Secchi depth stations were about 16 km from the trips, so the data cannot see water clarity, which is different from clarity not mattering.
  • "Caught anything" is a crude outcome. It says nothing about size or number, and fishing mode and target species move it far more than weather does.
  • Findings with a p value between 0.01 and 0.05 outside the main model are leads, not results. The only near-signal was striped bass in the minor solunar window, and with this many comparisons I treat it as chance.

Method in brief

I used a logistic regression with a light L2 penalty, chosen by cross-validation grouped by date. Intervals come from 100 bootstraps grouped by date, and p values are adjusted for the eight inputs tested. Moon and solunar timings were computed locally with Skyfield and the JPL DE421 ephemeris, and the computed moon position matched the database to 0.021 degrees at the median. Conditions come from the nearest valid NOAA station within 60 km of each trip, matched separately for each input. The data sources page credits every dataset.

The starting weights came from the MIT-licensed fishin project by Steve Wasko, as credited on the data sources page. I have since replaced most of them.
